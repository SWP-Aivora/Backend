Architecture Review: Aivora

Deepening opportunities for the AITasker backend.

Deepening Candidates

Candidate 1: The Contract Lifecycle Module

Strong

Files: HiringWorkflowService, JobService, ProposalService, ProjectService

Problem: The transition from a job post to an active project is conceptually fragmented. Status updates for Job, Proposal, and Project are manually orchestrated in shallow services. Callers have to jump between many small modules to understand the contract state.

Solution: Consolidate the lifecycle transitions into a deep Contract or Hiring module. The interface should accept an action (e.g., AcceptProposal(proposalId)) and handle the entire transactional state change internally.

Benefits: High Locality for contract invariants. Higher Leverage for controllers. The interface becomes the primary test surface for the entire workflow.

Candidate 2: The Unified Treasury Module

Strong

Files: Treasury, FinancialLedger, DisputeService, MilestoneService

Problem: Financial logic is split across a shallow seam. Treasury coordinates while FinancialLedger does math, but both are coupled to project entities. Worse, DisputeService and MilestoneService manually freeze/unfreeze payments, leaking financial invariants.

Solution: Merge Treasury and FinancialLedger into a single deep Treasury module. It should own the Payment lifecycle completely. Other services (Dispute, Milestone) should only signal intent (e.g., LockFundsForDispute(milestoneId)) rather than mutating statuses directly.

Benefits: Eliminates leaky financial seams. Concentrates "money math" bugs in one place. Provides massive leverage to any service touching escrow.

Candidate 3: Rich Entity Status Control

Worth exploring

Files: JobPost.cs, Milestone.cs, Project.cs

Problem: Entities are anemic. Status transitions (e.g., DRAFT -> OPEN) are validated in services, making services shallow and scattering business rules. The deletion test reveals that if we deleted these service checks, the invariants would be lost.

Solution: Move status transition logic and validation into the entities themselves (e.g., job.Publish()). Services then become simple orchestrators of persistence, rather than owners of business logic.

Benefits: Higher Depth for the domain model. Invariants are protected at the source. Understanding a status lifecycle only requires reading the entity.

Top Recommendation

Candidate 2: The Unified Treasury Module

The most critical friction in Aivora is the risk of inconsistent financial states. By merging Treasury and FinancialLedger into a single, deep module and forcing all other services through its interface, we ensure that money movements, payment statuses, and transaction logs are always in sync. This provides the highest Leverage and Locality for the project's most sensitive logic.
